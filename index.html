Workshop Overview

Deploying soft robotic technologies in challenging natural environments is a critical step toward realizing their full potential. Beyond drawing inspiration from nature, Soft Robotics in the Wild extends to one of humanity’s most fundamental needs: food production and agriculture. Crops are delicate and variable, fields are unstructured, and labour is scarce. Yet soft robots that work in the wild will not be hand-tuned; they will be designed, trained, and adapted by AI. Soft robotics offers physical compliance, conformability, and damage-free interaction with produce, but conventional design and control pipelines struggle with the nonlinear mechanics, high-dimensional design spaces, and complex contact dynamics of soft contact in the food industry. Modern AI – spanning neural surrogate physics modelling, multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, reinforcement learning, and data-driven visual-tactile perception – offers unprecedented opportunities to automate soft robotic synthesis and enable real-time control.

This half-day workshop explores the synergy between AI methodologies and soft mechatronics for field agriculture across three interconnected domains: Computational Design for Agriculture, Intelligent Perception and Tactile Sensing in Agricultural Deployment, and Learning-Based Control for Real-World Agricultural Deployment. Together, these domains aim to translate laboratory advances into harvesting, crop handling, post-harvest operations, and food waste processing. The programme features keynote talks from leading experts, technical presentations, and a highly interactive demo session, creating a vibrant environment for intellectual exchange and collaboration.

Invited Speakers

Prof. Chao Chen

Monash University, Australia

Prof. Chao Chen is a Professor in the Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering at Monash University, Australia. He established the Laboratory of Motion Generation and Analysis (LMGA) in 2008 and has served as its director since then. His research focuses on soft robotics, robotic motion and planning, and robotic intelligence, with applications in agriculture, healthcare, and infrastructure. His Monash Apple Retrieving System (MARS) project received the 2023 Excellence Award (Project of the Year) from Engineers Australia Victoria.

Asst. Prof. Tan Yu Jun

National University of Singapore

Dr. Tan Yu Jun is an Assistant Professor at the National University of Singapore, where she joined in January 2021. She received her BEng and PhD from Nanyang Technological University (NTU), specializing in additive manufacturing and biomaterials. Her research focuses on advanced manufacturing technologies, stretchable and self-healing electronics, and functional materials for developing soft machines and electronic systems. She has published over 20 peer-reviewed journal articles in leading journals, including Nature Materials, Nature Electronics, Nature Communications, Science Robotics, and Advanced Materials.

Dr. Yue Xie

Loughborough University, UK

Dr Yue Xie is a Lecturer in Computer Science at Loughborough University and an Associate Fellow of Advance HE (AFHEA). Her research develops artificial intelligence and optimisation methods for adaptive robotic and intelligent systems, with particular interests in evolutionary computation, embodied and physical AI, robotics and automation, optimisation under uncertainty, and autonomous engineering design.

Asst. Prof. Josie Hughes

EPFL, Lausanne, Switzerland

Dr. Josie Hughes is a Tenure Track Assistant Professor at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL), Switzerland, where she leads the Computational Robot Design and Fabrication (CREATE) Lab. Her research focuses on computational design, advanced fabrication, and data-driven optimization of robotic systems, including complex, multi-material, sensorized structures. Her work also explores robotic applications in agriculture, including AI-assisted gripper design and robotic fruit harvesting. She received the IEEE Robotics and Automation Society Early Academic Career Award in 2024.

Dr. Costanza Armanini

NYU Abu Dhabi, UAE

Dr Costanza Armanini received a BSc (2011) and MSc (2014) degrees in civil engineering and a PhD degree (2018) in mechanical engineering from the University of Trento, Italy. Between 2019 and 2023, she was a Postdoctoral Fellow with the Department of Mechanical Engineering, at Khalifa University, Abu Dhabi, UAE. In 2023 she joined the Center for Artificial Intelligence and Robotics, New York University Abu Dhabi, as a Research Associate. Her research interests include the modeling and design of soft robotics systems, with diverse application fields, including marine robotics and biomedical technologies.

Asst. Prof. Thilina Lalitharatne

Queen Mary University of London, UK

Dr. Thilina Dulantha Lalitharatne is a Lecturer (Assistant Professor) in Robotics and Mechatronics at the School of Engineering and Material Science in Queen Mary University of London (QMUL), UK. Before joining QMUL, he was a postdoctoral research associate attached to Morph Lab, Dyson School of Design Engineering, Imperial College London. Prior to this, he has worked as a Senior Lecturer in the Department of Mechanical Engineering, University of Moratuwa (UOM), Sri Lanka. Thilina earned his PhD in Robotics and Intelligent Systems from Saga University, Japan.

Asst. Prof. Marcello Calisti

Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna, Italy

Dr. Marcello Calisti is an Assistant Professor of Bioengineering at the BioRobotics Institute, Scuola Superiore Sant’Anna, Italy, where he has served since January 2018. He received his B.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Perugia in 2005 and his M.Sc. in Biomedical Engineering from the University of Florence in 2008. He completed his PhD at the BioRobotics Institute, where he subsequently worked as a Research Fellow in Prof. Cecilia Laschi’s laboratory from 2013 to 2017. His academic activities include research, supervision, teaching, and collaboration on European and national research projects.
